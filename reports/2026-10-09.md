# Literature update: 2026-10-09

This update adds **9 papers**, bringing the English index from 36 to **45**. Six were first submitted after the 2026-09-22 review; three earlier papers fill coverage gaps. All additions have primary arXiv abstract and submission-history checks. Selected method and evaluation sections were also inspected for Robo-Harness K1, Scale and Selection, and RegenHarness; none is labeled as a complete full-text review or independent reproduction. No cross-paper leaderboard is inferred.

## New submissions since the previous review

- **[VeriFine](../papers/2610.08761.md)** — submitted 2026-10-06, v1. Adds a policy/curriculum/judge improvement loop with human calibration. Human effort and independent judge evaluation remain part of the comparison. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08761v1).
- **[Language Model Agent Machine](../papers/2610.02488.md)** — submitted 2026-10-01, revised 2026-10-06, v2. Adds resource accounting for memory, model calls, and verification. This is a general-agent transfer reference, not robotics evidence. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02488v2).
- **[Scale and Selection](../papers/2609.39304.md)** — submitted 2026-09-30, v1. Studies rollout scale and incumbent-versus-candidate selection in harness optimization. Section 4 uses held-out tabletop tasks, whereas its RoboCasa campaign has no held-out set; Section 5 identifies simulation-only evaluation. [Primary source, Sections 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39304v1).
- **[Robo-Harness K1](../papers/2609.29389.md)** — submitted 2026-09-24, v1. Adds calibrated perception tools and training from tool-call traces. Appendix C.3's frontier comparison uses 18 matched cases and changes several interface components together; it is not an isolated depth ablation or a directly comparable literature leaderboard. [Primary source, Appendix C.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29389v1).
- **[RegenHarness](../papers/2609.27612.md)** — submitted 2026-09-23, v1. Adds evidence-controlled task progress and reviewed configuration updates. Sections 5–6 describe a single-host runtime and physical quadruped cases; these do not alone quantify general continual-improvement gains. [Primary source, Sections 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27612v1).
- **[123 Rounds of Skill Discovery](../papers/2609.31760.md)** — submitted 2026-09-23, v1. Adds a negative-result technical report: local improvements did not complete the target task. The case motivates better evaluators and coverage of later skills, without establishing a universal failure of self-improvement. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31760v1).

- **[RoboHarness: policy orchestration](../papers/2607.18060.md)** — first submitted 2026-07-20, latest v3 dated 2026-09-29. Adds capability-based routing and policy handoffs. The first-submission date comes from arXiv history, not a secondary listing's later publication date. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18060v3).
- **[RoboHarness: VLA adaptation](../papers/2603.24060.md)** — first submitted 2026-03-25, latest v3 dated 2026-08-12. Adds dual-memory retrieval and interventions around frozen policies. This is a separate work from the orchestration paper despite the shared name. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24060v3).

## Research implications

The following are maintainer hypotheses, not newly established results:

1. Evaluate update selection and verifier quality together. A stricter promotion rule can still optimize the wrong task criterion; a changing judge can also make apparent improvement difficult to interpret. Keep independent outcome labels and a frozen retention set.
2. Track which stages of a long task are actually reached. End-to-end success alone can hide missing evidence for later skills; controlled intermediate-state starts may separate coverage from skill quality.
3. Separate perception access, motion interface, memory, and completion feedback in interface experiments. A matched task subset does not by itself isolate which changed component produced a gain.
4. Account for rollout scale and human calibration alongside model inference. Simulation throughput and abstract checkpoint costs do not directly translate into physical robot deployment budgets.
